# 36 — The look

The app was drawn in a Modernist system: 2px ink rules, flush-left buttons,
uppercase labels, squared code tiles, a page tint of pale blue. It was
disciplined and it read as a document. The owner brought a reference — a
travel app of white cards on a pale ground, a teal header with a search
field, round doors in a row, photographs that scroll sideways, capsule
buttons — and asked for the app to be that easy to use and that pleasant to
look at. This is that pass.

## What changed

**The tokens.** Brand is teal (`#0e7480`), with a deep and a soft; the page
is a pale teal-grey, the neutral ramp is tinted to match, and the inverted
panel is deep teal. Nothing else in the palette moved: the evidence green,
the counter-accent red, the gold of Trip Points, the orange call-to-action
tokens are all as they were, and the contrast tests still measure every
pair. A `shadow.card` token joins the two shadows that existed, because a
white card on a white page needs one.

**Buttons** are capsules with a centred label; the primary is brand with a
white label from `onFill`. The flush-left signature is retired, in the
button's own header comment, for the reason there.

**The tab bar** is icons on white, the active one in brand, no filled block.

**Home** is the reference's shape with the island's content in it: a teal
header with the greeting and the island's name, the verified purse as a
chip, and a search field that asks the concierge — because a search over
five places is not worth a field, and the concierge is what a traveller
reaching for search actually wants. The conditions card lifts over the
header's bottom edge with the same measured average and the same
provenance line. Seven round doors scroll sideways. The measured places
scroll sideways as cards with their licensed photographs and the map pin's
score chip in the map pin's colours. Then today's missions, then what you
are carrying. No name in the greeting: the app collects none, and "Hi,
Paul" on a screen that has never asked who you are is the first lie the
reference tells.

**Missions** has pill filters and cards; the quest code sits in a soft
brand tile rather than an ink square. **Wallet** has its purses, its level
and its companions in cards.

## What did not change

Green still means a host verified something and nothing else wears it; a
Trip Point reward is still gold; a quest under way is still outlined in
brand and not in green. Every number on Home is still the measured one with
its provenance beside it, and every block still fails alone. The photographs
are still the licensed ones, credit and all, and a place without one shows
none.

## Still owed

- The remaining pushed screens (place, quest, safety, party, account) carry
  the tokens and the buttons but keep their rules and rows. They read as
  older than Home and Missions now, and each deserves the card treatment.
- Onboarding is the reference's strongest screen — a photograph and a
  question — and this app's weakest. It wants the licensed photography.
